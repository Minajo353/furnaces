How iron is made:
Blast Furnace vs Midrex

Two very different ways to turn iron ore into the iron that becomes steel. One melts it with coke. The other removes the oxygen with gas, without ever melting it.

The blast furnace

A giant vertical furnace, often 30 m or more tall, that runs non-stop for years. It is the main route for making steel worldwide, called BF-BOF.

Simplified cross-section (illustration, not to scale).

  1. ChargingIron ore (lumps, sinter or pellets), coke and limestone are loaded in layers at the top.
  2. Hot blastAir heated to about 1,000–1,200 °C is blown in near the bottom through nozzles called tuyeres.
  3. Making the gasCoke burns and forms carbon monoxide (CO), the gas that does the chemical work.
  4. ReductionCO strips oxygen from the ore as it sinks: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.
  5. Melting and tappingThe iron melts and collects in the hearth. Limestone binds impurities into slag, which floats on top. Both are tapped out regularly.

The Midrex furnace

A direct reduction process. Iron ore pellets slide down a shaft while hot reducing gas removes their oxygen. The ore never melts, and the product is called DRI (direct reduced iron).

Simplified diagram (illustration, not to scale).

  1. ChargingIron oxide pellets or lump ore enter the top of a vertical shaft.
  2. Making the gasNatural gas is reformed with recycled top gas into a hot mix of hydrogen (H₂) and carbon monoxide (CO).
  3. Reduction in solid stateThe gas enters the shaft at around 900 °C and removes oxygen from the pellets as they descend: Fe₂O₃ + 3H₂ → 2Fe + 3H₂O.
  4. Gas recyclingTop gas leaves the shaft, is cleaned, and is fed back into the reformer.
  5. DRI outputSponge-like DRI leaves the bottom, typically 92–95% metallic iron. It usually goes to an electric arc furnace to become steel.

Iron ores and chemistry

Iron is found in nature as oxides, so making iron means removing the oxygen. This is called reduction.

Raw materials

Ore also contains gangue, unwanted minerals such as silica, alumina, phosphorus and sulfur. Low-grade ore is crushed and concentrated, then rolled into pellets of about 65% iron or more.

The blast furnace also needs coke (fuel and reducing agent) and limestone (to form slag). Midrex needs very high-grade pellets, because the iron is never melted and there is no slag to carry the waste away.

Blast furnace reactions

  • C + O₂ → CO₂ (heat)
  • CO₂ + C → 2CO
  • 3Fe₂O₃ + CO → 2Fe₃O₄ + CO₂
  • Fe₃O₄ + CO → 3FeO + CO₂
  • FeO + CO → Fe + CO₂
  • CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
  • CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag)

Midrex reactions

The reformer turns natural gas into reducing gas:

  • CH₄ + CO₂ → 2CO + 2H₂
  • CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂

In the shaft, at roughly 800–950 °C:

  • Fe₂O₃ + 3H₂ → 2Fe + 3H₂O
  • Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

Why hydrogen matters

Iron melts at about 1,538 °C, so Midrex stays below that and the iron stays solid.

Carbon monoxide removes oxygen as CO₂. Hydrogen removes it as water (H₂O). That is why hydrogen-based reduction can cut emissions so much.

Environment and green steel

Ironmaking is a major source of CO₂. The route matters a lot.

In a blast furnace, carbon is the reducing agent, so CO₂ is a direct product of the chemistry. A DRI plant running on natural gas emits noticeably less, and running it on more hydrogen lowers emissions further. Pairing DRI with an electric arc furnace powered by renewable electricity is one of the main routes to so-called green steel. Real results depend on the plant, the gas and the electricity source.

A short history

From small clay furnaces to giant modern plants.

  • Ancient times: small bloomery furnaces make spongy iron that is hammered into shape.
  • Late Middle Ages: taller blast furnaces appear in Europe and produce liquid iron.
  • 1709: Abraham Darby smelts iron with coke at Coalbrookdale, England.
  • Late 1960s: the Midrex process is developed and the first commercial plant starts up.
  • Today: plants are testing hydrogen and other ways to cut CO₂.

Glossary

Reduction
Removing oxygen from an iron oxide.
Coke
Strong, porous carbon made from coal. It is the fuel and reducing agent in a blast furnace.
Slag
Molten waste made of lime and impurities. It floats on the iron.
Tuyere
A nozzle that blows hot air into the bottom of a blast furnace.
Gangue
Waste minerals mixed in with iron ore.
Sinter / pellet
Ore fines fused or rolled into lumps that let gas flow through the furnace.
DRI / HBI
Direct reduced iron, and the same product pressed into hot briquettes.
BOF
Basic oxygen furnace, which turns hot metal into steel.
EAF
Electric arc furnace, which melts DRI or scrap into steel.

Questions

Why does a blast furnace use coke instead of coal?

Coke is strong enough to hold up the weight of the load, and it stays porous so gas can flow through. Raw coal would crumble and block the gas.

Why does Midrex need high-grade ore?

The iron is not melted, so there is no slag to remove waste. Any gangue stays in the DRI and is dealt with later in the electric arc furnace, which costs more energy.

Can Midrex run on hydrogen?

Yes, plants can use increasing shares of hydrogen alongside natural gas. Running fully on hydrogen is possible in principle, but it depends on how much hydrogen is available and what it costs.

Which route is greener?

DRI with an electric arc furnace usually emits less CO₂ than a blast furnace, and even less with hydrogen and clean electricity. The exact difference depends on the plant.
